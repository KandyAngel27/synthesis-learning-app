## Lesson Plan (24 Lessons)

### Unit 1: Revenue Cycle & Reimbursement Foundations (L1-4)
1. **"The Revenue Cycle: How Healthcare Gets Paid"** — Complete revenue cycle overview (front-end, middle, back-end), why HIM is at the center (coding drives revenue), revenue cycle team roles (patient access, coders, billers, CDI specialists, A/R analysts, compliance officers), revenue integrity concept, financial impact of coding accuracy (case studies: one missed CC = thousands in lost revenue), healthcare as a business vs a mission — balancing both
   - Cover: revenue cycle flowchart, each step's purpose, HIM touchpoints

2. **"Healthcare Payer Landscape"** — Government payers: Medicare (Parts A/B/C/D, CMS administration, MACs), Medicaid (federal-state, FMAP), TRICARE, VA, CHIP, Workers' Comp. Commercial payers: Blue Cross Blue Shield, UnitedHealthcare, Aetna, Cigna, Humana — market structure. Self-funded employer plans (ERISA). Payer hierarchy and coordination of benefits (COB): primary, secondary, tertiary, birthday rule, MSP (Medicare Secondary Payer) rules. Payer mix analysis: impact on facility financial health, payer contracts and fee schedules, allowed amounts vs billed charges
   - Cover: payer comparison table, COB decision tree, MSP rules

3. **"Claim Forms: UB-04 & CMS-1500"** — UB-04 (CMS-1450): institutional claims, form locators (FL1-FL81), key fields (type of bill, revenue codes, HCPCS/CPT, ICD-10-CM/PCS, condition codes, occurrence codes, value codes, payer info, charges). CMS-1500: professional claims, 33 blocks, key fields (place of service, diagnosis pointers, CPT/HCPCS, modifiers, rendering provider NPI, referring provider, prior authorization). Electronic equivalents: 837I (institutional) and 837P (professional). Claim scrubbing: edits, error resolution, clean claim rate. When to use each form: facility-based vs physician-based billing
   - Cover: UB-04 field map SVG, CMS-1500 field map SVG, 837 transaction structure

4. **"Chargemaster & Charge Capture"** — Chargemaster/Charge Description Master (CDM): purpose, structure (charge code, description, CPT/HCPCS, revenue code, charge amount, department), maintenance (annual review, regulatory updates, new codes). Charge capture: how charges flow from departments to billing (automated vs manual, EHR integration, charge tickets/superbills). Common CDM errors: incorrect code mapping, outdated codes, duplicate entries, missing charges (charge leakage), exploding charges. CDM committee: composition (HIM, finance, compliance, clinical departments), review frequency, regulatory triggers (new CPT codes annually, OPPS updates, IPPS updates). Revenue code mapping to CPT/HCPCS
   - Cover: CDM structure diagram, charge capture workflow, common error types

### Unit 2: Prospective Payment Systems — Inpatient (L5-9)
5. **"MS-DRGs & the Inpatient PPS (IPPS)"** — History: TEFRA 1982, Social Security Amendments 1983, implemented October 1983. How IPPS works: per-discharge payment, MS-DRG assignment drives payment. MS-DRG structure: 764 MS-DRGs (FY2024), organized into 25 Major Diagnostic Categories (MDCs) based on principal diagnosis, medical vs surgical partitioning. Pre-MDC DRGs (transplants, tracheostomy, ECMO). DRG assignment process: principal diagnosis → MDC → medical or surgical → base DRG → CC/MCC severity split → final MS-DRG. Grouper software (Medicare Code Editor + MS-DRG Grouper)
   - Cover: DRG assignment flowchart, MDC list, medical vs surgical partition decision tree

6. **"MS-DRG Payment Calculation"** — Payment formula: Base rate × DRG relative weight × Wage index adjustment = Payment. Base rate components: operating (labor + non-labor) + capital. Wage index: adjusts for geographic cost differences, CBSA (Core-Based Statistical Area). Relative weight: resource intensity (1.0000 = average, higher = more resources). Add-on payments: IME (Indirect Medical Education — teaching hospitals), DSH (Disproportionate Share Hospital — safety net hospitals), outlier payments (high-cost cases: fixed-loss threshold, marginal cost factor 80%). Transfer policy: per-diem payment for short stays, DRG-specific GMLOS, post-acute care transfer policy. New technology add-on payments (NTAP). Worked examples with real numbers
   - Cover: Payment formula SVG with worked example, wage index map concept, add-on payment decision tree

7. **"CC/MCC Impact & Clinical Documentation Integrity"** — CC (Complication or Comorbidity) vs MCC (Major CC) vs non-CC: severity tiers and payment impact. CC/MCC exclusion list (when a CC is closely related to principal diagnosis, it's excluded). Example: DRG 291 (Heart Failure w/MCC, RW 1.2297) vs DRG 292 (HF w/CC, RW 0.8714) vs DRG 293 (HF w/o CC/MCC, RW 0.6531) — same principal diagnosis, huge payment difference. CDI programs: purpose (accurate severity capture, not upcoding), concurrent review workflow, physician query process (compliant queries: non-leading, open-ended, provide clinical indicators, reference documentation), CDI specialist role, query templates, CDI metrics (query rate, agreement rate, CC/MCC capture rate, case mix index impact). Revenue impact calculations
   - Cover: CC/MCC severity ladder SVG, CDI query workflow, payment comparison table

8. **"Case Mix Index (CMI) Analysis"** — CMI formula: sum of all DRG relative weights ÷ total discharges. What CMI tells you: average resource intensity, severity of illness, documentation/coding accuracy indicator. CMI trending: month-over-month, year-over-year, by service line, by physician. CMI benchmarking: compare to peer facilities (bed size, teaching status, region). Factors affecting CMI: patient acuity, coding accuracy, CDI effectiveness, documentation quality, payer mix. CMI and budget: CMI drives Medicare payment, used in cost reporting, impacts DSH eligibility. Adjusted vs unadjusted CMI: removing outlier DRGs. CMI auditing: identifying CMI drift, compliance implications, coding accuracy reviews. Worked CMI calculations with multiple scenarios
   - Cover: CMI formula SVG, CMI trending graph, benchmark comparison, worked calculations

9. **"Coding Audits & DRG Validation"** — Audit types: prospective (pre-bill), concurrent (during stay), retrospective (post-bill). Internal audits: coding accuracy review, sample methodology (random, targeted, focused), audit tools and scorecards, coder feedback. External audits: RAC (Recovery Audit Contractor — look-back period, contingency fee, auto-denial vs complex review), CERT (Comprehensive Error Rate Testing — statistical validity), MAC audits, OIG audits, PEPPER reports (Program for Evaluating Payment Patterns Electronic Report — identifies outlier areas). DRG validation: re-abstracting, principal diagnosis verification, CC/MCC validation, POA accuracy, procedure code verification. Responding to audit findings: appeals process (5 Medicare appeal levels), corrective action plans, coder education. Coding accuracy rate calculation and benchmarks (target >95%)
   - Cover: Audit type comparison table, RAC process flowchart, PEPPER target areas, appeals levels

### Unit 3: Prospective Payment Systems — Outpatient & Other Settings (L10-14)
10. **"OPPS & Ambulatory Payment Classifications (APCs)"** — Outpatient Prospective Payment System: per-service payment (not per-visit like DRGs). APC structure: clinically and resource-similar services grouped, relative weight, national payment rate. APC types: Comprehensive C-APCs (packages related services into single APC), Composite APCs (mental health, T-drug administration), standard APCs. Status indicators (CRITICAL for exam): S (significant procedure, not discounted), T (significant procedure, multiple procedure reduction), V (clinic/ED visit), N (packaged into other APC), Q (T-packaged, conditional packaging), J1/J2 (comprehensive APC). Packaging: items/services always packaged (labs, supplies, anesthesia) vs conditional packaging. Payment calculation: APC relative weight × conversion factor × wage index. Outlier payments in OPPS
   - Cover: APC status indicator table, packaging rules, C-APC diagram, payment calculation

11. **"Physician Fee Schedule & RVUs"** — Medicare Physician Fee Schedule (MPFS): Resource-Based Relative Value Scale (RBRVS). RVU components: Work RVU (physician time, skill, intensity) + Practice Expense RVU (overhead) + Malpractice RVU. Each adjusted by GPCI (Geographic Practice Cost Index). Payment formula: [(Work RVU × Work GPCI) + (PE RVU × PE GPCI) + (MP RVU × MP GPCI)] × Conversion Factor ($33.29 in 2024). Global vs professional vs technical billing: -26 modifier (professional only), -TC modifier (technical only), no modifier (global/both). MIPS impact on MPFS payment (up to +/-9% adjustment). Facility vs non-facility PE RVUs (facility PE is lower because hospital bears overhead). Worked examples comparing facility vs non-facility payment
   - Cover: RVU formula SVG with all components, GPCI concept, facility vs non-facility comparison

12. **"SNF, Home Health & Rehab Payment Systems"** — SNF PPS: PDPM (Patient-Driven Payment Model, replaced RUG-IV October 2019), 5 case-mix adjusted components (PT, OT, SLP, nursing, non-therapy ancillary), each with its own classification, per-diem payment, variable per-diem adjustment, MDS 3.0 drives classification, ICD-10 diagnosis-driven (not therapy minutes). Home Health PPS: PDGM (Patient-Driven Groupings Model, January 2020), 30-day payment periods (replaced 60-day episodes), 432 payment groups based on: admission source (community vs institutional), timing (early vs late), clinical grouping (12 groups from ICD-10), functional level (from OASIS), comorbidity (none, low, high). IRF PPS: CMG (Case-Mix Group), IRF-PAI assessment, 60% rule (must have qualifying conditions). LTCH PPS: MS-LTC-DRGs, 25+ day average LOS
   - Cover: PDPM 5-component diagram, PDGM classification tree, IRF 60% rule, setting comparison table

13. **"ASC, Hospice & Other Payment Systems"** — ASC Payment System: ASC-covered surgical procedures list, ASC payment groups (separate from hospital OPPS), device-intensive procedures (adjusted payment), separately payable drugs, ASC quality reporting. Hospice: 4 levels of care (routine home care, continuous home care, inpatient respite, general inpatient), per-diem payment rates, election benefit period (two 90-day + unlimited 60-day), hospice cap ($33,494 in FY2024), hospice quality reporting. Other systems: FQHC PPS (per-visit), Critical Access Hospital (CAH — cost-based reimbursement, 101% of reasonable costs), Maryland waiver (all-payer rate setting, global budgets), Indian Health Service. Payment system comparison table across ALL settings
   - Cover: Hospice 4 levels SVG, ASC vs HOPD payment comparison, comprehensive PPS comparison table

14. **"Managed Care Reimbursement & Contracting"** — Managed care payment models: capitation (PMPM — per member per month), discounted fee-for-service, per-diem rates, case rates, percentage of charges. Contract negotiation: fee schedules, carve-outs, stop-loss provisions, withhold arrangements, risk corridors. Medicare Advantage: CMS capitation to plans (risk-adjusted using HCCs), bid process, benchmarks, star ratings impact on bonus payments. Medicaid managed care: states contract with MCOs, capitation rates set by actuaries, rate certification. Provider network adequacy requirements. Value-based contract elements: shared savings, shared risk, quality bonuses, episode-based payment. Risk adjustment: HCC model, RAF scores, diagnosis-driven payment, importance of accurate coding for risk capture
   - Cover: Capitation calculation example, risk adjustment diagram, MA star rating tiers, contract type comparison

### Unit 4: Advanced Coding & Reimbursement Integration (L15-19)
15. **"Coding's Direct Impact on Reimbursement"** — Connecting codes to dollars: ICD-10-CM drives DRG assignment (inpatient), ICD-10-PCS drives surgical DRG partition, CPT/HCPCS drives APC assignment (outpatient) and MPFS payment (physician). Case studies: Same patient, different coding = different DRGs = $5,000-$15,000 payment difference. Specificity matters: unspecified codes often map to lower-paying DRGs. POA impact: HAC program reduces payment for hospital-acquired conditions. Sequencing impact: principal diagnosis determines MDC, secondary diagnoses add CCs/MCCs. Query opportunities: documentation gaps that cost revenue (specificity, acuity, laterality, causal relationships). CC/MCC capture rate analysis by service line
   - Cover: Code-to-payment flow diagram, case study comparison table, revenue impact calculations

16. **"Denial Management & Appeals"** — Denial categories: clinical (medical necessity, authorization), technical (eligibility, timely filing, duplicate claims), coding (incorrect codes, unbundling, sequencing). Denial analysis: root cause, denial rate by payer/reason/department, trending. Prevention strategies: front-end verification, pre-authorization workflows, clean claim submission, coding accuracy. Appeal process: internal review → payer appeal → external review. Medicare 5-level appeal process: Level 1 redetermination (MAC, 120 days), Level 2 reconsideration (QIC, 180 days), Level 3 ALJ hearing ($180 threshold, 60 days), Level 4 Medicare Appeals Council (60 days), Level 5 federal district court ($1,760 threshold, 60 days). Commercial appeal processes. Writing effective appeal letters: clinical documentation, medical necessity arguments, coding references. Denial write-off vs appeal decision (cost-benefit analysis)
   - Cover: Denial category breakdown SVG, 5-level appeals flowchart with timelines/thresholds, appeal letter components

17. **"Compliance in Coding & Reimbursement"** — OIG Work Plan: annual audit priorities relevant to coding/reimbursement (specific targets like evaluation of MS-DRG 981, sepsis coding, outpatient observations). OIG compliance guidance for hospitals: 7 elements applied to coding. Common compliance risks: upcoding (coding higher severity than documented), unbundling (separately billing component codes), DRG creep (systematic shift to higher-paying DRGs without acuity change), phantom charges, duplicate billing, misrepresenting discharge status, POA falsification. False Claims Act liability for coders and facilities. Compliance auditing: internal monitoring, external benchmarking, PEPPER report analysis (identify your outlier areas before RAC does). Safe harbors and red flags. Whistleblower/qui tam: protections and implications. AHIMA Standards of Ethical Coding (12 standards — know key ones)
   - Cover: OIG Work Plan priority areas, compliance risk identification chart, AHIMA ethical coding standards

18. **"Healthcare Financial Analysis for HIM"** — Budget basics: operating budget (revenue and expenses) vs capital budget (equipment, IT systems, >$500-$5,000 threshold varies). HIM budget components: salaries (coders, analysts, CDI), software (encoder, CAC, CDI), outsourcing costs, education/credentials. Cost accounting: direct vs indirect costs, fixed vs variable, cost-to-charge ratio (CCR), cost per unit of service. Financial metrics HIM professionals must know: revenue per discharge, cost per case, profit margin, operating margin, days cash on hand. ROI calculations for HIM initiatives: CDI program ROI (investment in CDI specialist salary vs CMI improvement × case volume × average payment increase), coding automation ROI, outsourcing cost-benefit. Financial reporting: monthly operating reports, variance analysis (favorable vs unfavorable)
   - Cover: Budget template SVG, ROI calculation example, cost accounting formulas, variance analysis table

19. **"National Health Expenditure & Policy Impact"** — U.S. healthcare spending: $4.5 trillion (2022), 17.3% of GDP, per capita $13,493. Spending by category: hospital care (31%), physician services (20%), prescription drugs (9%), nursing care (5%), government administration (8%). Spending by payer: private insurance (31%), Medicare (21%), Medicaid (17%), out-of-pocket (11%), other (20%). Cost drivers: aging population, technology advancement, chronic disease prevalence, administrative complexity, drug pricing. Policy impacts on reimbursement: sequestration (2% Medicare payment reduction), IPPS annual updates (market basket minus productivity adjustment), physician fee schedule conversion factor changes, site-neutral payment debates. Future trends: bundled payments expansion, total cost of care models, price transparency (Hospital Price Transparency Rule, No Surprises Act 2022), global budgets (Maryland model)
   - Cover: NHE pie charts (by category, by payer), spending trend line, policy impact timeline

### Unit 5: Emerging Models & Capstone (L20-24)
20. **"Value-Based Purchasing & Quality-Linked Payment"** — CMS Hospital VBP: ACA Section 3001, up to 2% payment at risk, 4 domains (clinical outcomes, person/community engagement, safety, efficiency/cost reduction). HRRP: ACA Section 3025, up to 3% penalty for excess 30-day readmissions (6 conditions: AMI, HF, pneumonia, COPD, hip/knee, CABG). HAC Reduction Program: bottom quartile gets 1% payment reduction, measures include PSI-90 composite, CLABSI, CAUTI, SSI, MRSA, C. diff. Hospital IQR: pay-for-reporting (must report quality data or 25% payment reduction). MIPS connection: Quality (30%), Promoting Interoperability (25%), Improvement Activities (15%), Cost (30%). How HIM drives VBP success: accurate coding → accurate quality measures, POA accuracy → HAC program, CDI → severity capture → risk adjustment → expected mortality/readmission rates
   - Cover: VBP domain diagram, HRRP penalty calculation, HAC measure list, HIM-to-VBP connection map

21. **"Risk Adjustment & HCC Coding"** — Risk adjustment purpose: predict future healthcare costs based on diagnoses, adjust payments to reflect patient acuity. CMS-HCC model: Hierarchical Condition Categories, 86 HCCs (2024 V28 model), diagnosis → HCC mapping, hierarchy application (highest-severity HCC in a family counts), additive model. RAF score: sum of demographic factors + HCC weights = Risk Adjustment Factor, applied to capitation payment (Medicare Advantage). Coding for risk adjustment: must be from face-to-face encounter, documented by acceptable provider type, supported by medical record. Annual documentation requirements: all chronic conditions must be documented AND coded every year. Risk adjustment audits: RADV (Risk Adjustment Data Validation), extrapolation methodology, FCA implications for unsupported diagnoses. Risk adjustment in other contexts: ACA marketplace risk adjustment, Medicaid managed care. HIM role: accurate outpatient coding, physician education, risk adjustment coding reviews
   - Cover: HCC model diagram, RAF score calculation, diagnosis-to-HCC mapping example, annual documentation requirement

22. **"Alternative Payment Models & Bundled Payments"** — APM spectrum: FFS → pay-for-reporting → pay-for-performance → shared savings → shared risk → bundled → capitation → global budget. BPCI Advanced: 29 clinical episodes, 90-day window (anchor stay/procedure + post-acute), CMS sets target price, provider takes upside/downside risk, quality thresholds. CJR (Comprehensive Joint Replacement): mandatory geographic model for hip/knee, 90-day episodes. Oncology Care Model → Enhancing Oncology Model. ACO models: MSSP tracks (Basic Levels A-E, Enhanced), ACO REACH (equity focus, global/professional capitation). Direct Contracting/REACH: primary care capitation + total cost of care. Coding implications: accurate coding drives target price accuracy, risk-stratification, quality measure denominators. HIM in APMs: retrospective chart review, coding validation, episode analytics
   - Cover: APM risk spectrum SVG, BPCI episode timeline, ACO track comparison, coding's role in each model

23. **"Emerging Trends in Reimbursement"** — Price transparency: Hospital Price Transparency Rule (machine-readable files, 300 shoppable services, CMS penalties), No Surprises Act (2022, protect patients from surprise out-of-network bills, IDR process, good faith estimates for uninsured, NSA and HIM — new documentation/coding requirements). AI in revenue cycle: computer-assisted coding (CAC), NLP for CDI, automated charge capture, predictive denial management, autonomous coding (emerging). Prior authorization reform: CMS interoperability rules (electronic prior auth, FHIR-based APIs). Site-neutral payment: equalizing payment between HOPD and physician office for same services. Global budgets (Maryland model expanding), total cost of care contracts. Consumer-directed healthcare: HSAs, HDHPs, patient financial responsibility growth. Blockchain for claims processing (concept). HIM professional evolution: from coder to revenue integrity analyst, data analyst, CDI director
   - Cover: Price transparency requirements, AI in revenue cycle diagram, emerging role evolution

24. **"Capstone: Integrated Coding & Reimbursement Case Studies"** — Complex multi-setting case studies: Patient journey from ED → inpatient → SNF → home health — code each setting, identify payment system, calculate expected reimbursement. Dual coding exercise: same patient coded at inpatient (ICD-10-CM/PCS → MS-DRG → payment) AND if treated outpatient (ICD-10-CM + CPT → APC → payment). CDI impact case: before and after CDI query — DRG shift, payment difference, CMI impact. Denial scenario: code the case, receive denial, write appeal, identify root cause. Compliance audit scenario: identify coding errors in a sample, calculate accuracy rate, recommend corrective actions. Revenue cycle KPI dashboard interpretation. Career pathways: CCS, CCS-P, CDIP, CHDA, revenue cycle director, CDI manager, compliance officer
   - Cover: Multi-setting patient flow SVG, payment comparison tables, KPI dashboard mockup

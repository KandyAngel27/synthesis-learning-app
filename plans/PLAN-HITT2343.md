## Lesson Plan (22 Lessons)

### Unit 1: Quality Foundations (L1-4)
1. **"The Quality Imperative in Healthcare"** — Why quality matters: IOM "To Err Is Human" (1999, 44,000-98,000 preventable deaths/year, equivalent to a jumbo jet crash every day), "Crossing the Quality Chasm" (2001, 6 aims — STEEEP). Quality defined: degree to which health services increase likelihood of desired health outcomes, consistent with current professional knowledge (IOM). Brief history: Codman (end result system, 1910s), Donabedian (structure-process-outcome, 1960s), Deming (continuous improvement, 1980s), IOM reports (1999-2001), Patient Safety Movement (2000s-present). Quality stakeholders: patients, providers, payers, regulators, accreditors, employers, public. Quality vs safety vs patient experience: interrelated but distinct. The business case for quality: reduced readmissions, fewer complications, higher reimbursement (VBP), lower malpractice, better reputation, staff satisfaction
   - Cover: STEEEP mnemonic SVG, quality history timeline, stakeholder map

2. **"Quality Theories & Frameworks"** — Donabedian Model: Structure (what you have — facilities, equipment, staffing, credentials, policies), Process (what you do — clinical care, documentation, coding, communication), Outcome (what happens — mortality, morbidity, patient satisfaction, functional status, readmission). Application: structure enables process, process leads to outcome. W. Edwards Deming: 14 Points for Management, system of profound knowledge, variation reduction, "drive out fear." Joseph Juran: quality trilogy (planning, control, improvement), Pareto principle (80/20). Philip Crosby: "quality is free," zero defects, cost of poor quality. Lean thinking (Toyota Production System): value stream, waste elimination (8 wastes: DOWNTIME — Defects, Overproduction, Waiting, Non-utilized talent, Transportation, Inventory, Motion, Extra-processing). Six Sigma: statistical process control, DMAIC (Define-Measure-Analyze-Improve-Control), 3.4 defects per million. Lean Six Sigma: combining waste elimination with variation reduction. Baldrige Excellence Framework: 7 criteria for performance excellence
   - Cover: Donabedian S-P-O model diagram, Deming's 14 Points list, 8 wastes (DOWNTIME) grid, DMAIC cycle

3. **"Quality Measurement: Types & Sources"** — Measure types: structure measures (do you have the capability?), process measures (did you do the right thing?), outcome measures (what happened?), balancing measures (did improvement cause harm elsewhere?). Measure attributes: validity, reliability, risk adjustment, feasibility. Data sources: administrative data (claims, UB-04, CMS-1500), clinical data (EHR, chart abstraction), patient-reported data (surveys, PROs), registry data. Numerator/denominator logic: inclusion/exclusion criteria, measurement period, risk adjustment methods. Rate vs ratio vs proportion: definitions and when to use each. Risk adjustment: why it matters (comparing apples to apples), methods (O/E ratios — observed/expected, logistic regression, APR-DRGs for severity), CMS risk adjustment (HCC model). Case mix index as quality proxy. Leading vs lagging indicators
   - Cover: Measure type comparison with examples, data source mapping, O/E ratio explanation SVG

4. **"National Quality Organizations & Infrastructure"** — AHRQ (Agency for Healthcare Research & Quality): patient safety research, Quality Indicators (PSI, IQI, PDI, PQI — definitions and examples of each), HCUP databases, TeamSTEPPS. NQF (National Quality Forum): endorses quality measures, Serious Reportable Events ("Never Events" — 29 events in 7 categories), consensus development process. IHI (Institute for Healthcare Improvement): Triple Aim → Quadruple Aim → Quintuple Aim (better health, better care, lower cost, provider well-being, health equity), 100,000 Lives Campaign, Open School. Leapfrog Group: hospital safety grades (A-F), employer-driven quality transparency, CPOE evaluation, ICU physician staffing. CMS: quality programs (Hospital Compare → Care Compare), star ratings, quality reporting programs. The Joint Commission: ORYX performance measurement, National Patient Safety Goals. State quality organizations: QIOs (Quality Improvement Organizations), state health department quality reporting. NCQA: health plan accreditation, HEDIS measures
   - Cover: Quality organization comparison table, AHRQ QI types grid, Triple → Quintuple Aim evolution

### Unit 2: Performance Improvement Tools & Methods (L5-9)
5. **"PDCA/PDSA & the Improvement Cycle"** — PDCA (Deming/Shewhart): Plan (identify problem, analyze root cause, develop countermeasure) → Do (implement on small scale, pilot) → Check (measure results, compare to prediction) → Act (adopt, adapt, or abandon). PDSA variation: Study replaces Check (emphasize learning, not just measurement). Key principles: small tests of change (rapid cycle), iterative (multiple PDSA cycles), prediction-based (predict outcome before testing), build knowledge sequentially. Model for Improvement (IHI): three fundamental questions (What are we trying to accomplish? How will we know a change is an improvement? What changes can we make that will result in improvement?) + PDSA cycles. FOCUS-PDCA: Find process to improve → Organize team → Clarify current knowledge → Understand variation → Select improvement → PDCA. Project charter: aim statement (SMART), team composition, scope, measures, timeline. Run charts to track improvement over time
   - Cover: PDCA cycle SVG, Model for Improvement 3 questions + PDSA, FOCUS-PDCA steps, run chart example

6. **"Root Cause Analysis & FMEA"** — Root Cause Analysis (RCA): retrospective analysis AFTER an adverse event/sentinel event, goal = identify system causes (not blame individuals). RCA process: charter the team → describe what happened (timeline, event flow) → identify contributing factors → identify root causes (drill to system level) → develop corrective actions → implement → measure effectiveness. Tools within RCA: 5 Whys (keep asking "why" until root cause), fishbone/Ishikawa diagram (6 categories: People, Process, Equipment, Materials, Environment, Management), event timeline, causal tree. Failure Mode and Effects Analysis (FMEA): PROACTIVE risk assessment BEFORE an adverse event. FMEA process: select process → assemble team → list process steps → identify failure modes (what could go wrong?) → determine effects → assign severity (1-10) → determine causes → assign occurrence (1-10) → identify current controls → assign detection (1-10) → calculate RPN (Severity × Occurrence × Detection) → prioritize high-RPN items → develop actions → re-assess. RCA vs FMEA: reactive vs proactive, when to use each. TJC requirements: sentinel event policy, RCA expected
   - Cover: RCA process flowchart, fishbone diagram template, FMEA RPN calculation SVG, RCA vs FMEA comparison

7. **"Statistical Process Control & Data Display"** — Common cause vs special cause variation: common = inherent in process (random, expected), special = assignable, identifiable (unusual, something changed). Control charts (Shewhart charts): center line (mean), upper control limit (UCL = mean + 3σ), lower control limit (LCL = mean - 3σ). Types: X-bar chart (continuous variable means), p-chart (proportion/percentage), c-chart (count of defects per unit), u-chart (rate of defects per unit), I-MR chart (individual measurements). Rules for detecting special cause: 1 point beyond 3σ, 8+ points on same side of center, 6+ points trending up/down, 2 of 3 consecutive beyond 2σ same side. Run charts: simpler than control charts, median line, rules for non-random patterns (shift = 6+ above/below median, trend = 5+ ascending/descending, too many/few runs). Pareto chart: 80/20 rule, bars in descending order + cumulative line, identifies vital few. Histogram: frequency distribution, shape (normal, skewed, bimodal). Scatter diagram: correlation visualization (positive, negative, none). Flowchart/process map: swimlane diagrams for cross-functional processes
   - Cover: Control chart anatomy SVG with UCL/LCL/mean, special cause rules illustrated, Pareto chart example, chart selection guide

8. **"Lean Healthcare & Waste Elimination"** — Lean principles: define value (from patient's perspective), map value stream, create flow, establish pull, pursue perfection. Value stream mapping: current state → identify waste → future state design. 8 wastes in healthcare (DOWNTIME): Defects (medication errors, wrong-site surgery), Overproduction (unnecessary tests, over-documentation), Waiting (patient wait times, delays in results, bed assignment), Non-utilized talent (coders doing clerical tasks, nurses doing non-nursing work), Transportation (unnecessary patient transport, specimen movement), Inventory (expired supplies, excess stock), Motion (searching for equipment, walking to distant printers), Extra-processing (redundant data entry, unnecessary approvals). Lean tools: 5S (Sort, Set in order, Shine, Standardize, Sustain), Kaizen events (rapid improvement, 3-5 day focused team), Gemba walks (go to where work happens), visual management (status boards, Kanban), A3 problem solving (one-page structured problem solving). Lean in HIM: reducing chart completion turnaround, streamlining ROI, coding workflow optimization, reducing claim denials
   - Cover: 8 wastes DOWNTIME grid with healthcare examples, 5S steps, value stream map example, A3 template

9. **"Six Sigma & DMAIC in Healthcare"** — Six Sigma: data-driven methodology to reduce defects to 3.4 per million opportunities, sigma levels (1σ=690K DPMO through 6σ=3.4 DPMO). DMAIC: Define (project charter, VOC — voice of customer, SIPOC — Suppliers/Inputs/Process/Outputs/Customers) → Measure (current state metrics, data collection plan, measurement system analysis, process capability — Cp/Cpk) → Analyze (root cause analysis, hypothesis testing, regression, correlation, process mapping) → Improve (brainstorm solutions, pilot testing, implementation planning, mistake-proofing/poka-yoke) → Control (control plan, control charts, standard work, monitoring dashboard, hand-off to process owner). Belt system: White Belt (awareness), Yellow Belt (project team member), Green Belt (leads projects part-time), Black Belt (leads complex projects full-time), Master Black Belt (trains/mentors). Lean Six Sigma: combines waste elimination (Lean) with variation reduction (Six Sigma). Healthcare applications: reducing surgical site infections, decreasing ED wait times, improving coding accuracy, reducing claim denials, improving patient throughput
   - Cover: Sigma level table (DPMO per level), DMAIC phase diagram with key tools per phase, SIPOC template, belt hierarchy

### Unit 3: Accreditation & Regulatory Quality (L10-13)
10. **"The Joint Commission & Hospital Accreditation"** — TJC history: 1951 JCAH → 1987 JCAHO → 2007 TJC. Deemed status: TJC accreditation satisfies CMS Conditions of Participation (most hospitals choose TJC over direct CMS survey). Survey process: unannounced (within 18-month window of 3-year cycle), tracer methodology (individual patient tracer follows a patient's care experience, system tracer examines systems like medication management, infection control, data management). Scoring: requirements (standards), elements of performance (EPs), measuring compliance. Standards chapters relevant to HIM: Record of Care (RC), Information Management (IM), Leadership (LD). National Patient Safety Goals (NPSGs): annual goals by program (hospital, ambulatory, nursing care, behavioral health), examples: patient identification (2 identifiers), communication (read-back, handoff — SBAR), medication safety (high-alert medications, labeling, reconciliation), infection prevention (hand hygiene, CLABSI, CAUTI, SSI), falls reduction, surgical site verification (Universal Protocol: pre-op verification, site marking, time-out). Sentinel events: unexpected occurrence involving death or serious harm, mandatory RCA, not required to report to TJC (encouraged), reviewable events list
   - Cover: TJC tracer methodology diagram, NPSG categories with examples, Universal Protocol 3-step process, sentinel event response flow

11. **"CMS Quality Programs & Value-Based Purchasing"** — CMS Hospital IQR (Inpatient Quality Reporting): must report quality data or face 25% payment reduction, chart-abstracted measures + eCQMs. Hospital VBP (Value-Based Purchasing): ACA Section 3001, up to 2% at risk (withheld from base payment, redistributed based on performance), 4 domains — Clinical Outcomes (mortality, complications), Safety (HAI measures from NHSN), Efficiency/Cost Reduction (Medicare spending per beneficiary), Person & Community Engagement (HCAHPS patient experience). HRRP (Hospital Readmissions Reduction Program): ACA Section 3025, up to 3% penalty for excess 30-day readmissions, 6 conditions (AMI, HF, pneumonia, COPD, hip/knee replacement, CABG), excess readmission ratio (ERR = predicted/expected), dual-eligible adjustment. HAC Reduction Program: bottom 25% of hospitals get 1% payment reduction, measures: CMS PSI-90 composite + CDC NHSN HAI measures (CLABSI, CAUTI, SSI colon/hysterectomy, MRSA bacteremia, C. diff). Hospital Star Ratings: 1-5 stars on Care Compare, 5 groups of measures. OQR (Outpatient Quality Reporting). How HIM drives quality scores: accurate coding → accurate risk adjustment → accurate expected rates
   - Cover: VBP 4 domains with weights SVG, HRRP penalty calculation, HAC measures list, HIM-to-quality connection map

12. **"Other Quality Reporting & Accreditation Programs"** — HEDIS (Healthcare Effectiveness Data and Information Set): NCQA, managed care quality, 90+ measures across 6 domains (effectiveness, access/availability, experience, utilization, health plan descriptive, measures reported using electronic clinical data), examples: breast cancer screening, diabetes A1c control, antidepressant medication management. Core measures / eCQMs: CMS Joint Commission aligned measures, electronic clinical quality measures submitted from EHR, measure specifications (numerator, denominator, exclusions). CAHPS (Consumer Assessment of Healthcare Providers and Systems): standardized patient experience surveys, HCAHPS (hospital, 29 questions, 8 composites: nurse communication, doctor communication, responsiveness, pain management, medication communication, discharge information, care transition, hospital environment), CG-CAHPS (clinician/group), HHCAHPS (home health). Other accreditors: DNV GL (ISO 9001-based, continuous improvement culture, annual surveys), CARF (rehabilitation, 3-year cycle, person-centered standards), AAAHC (ambulatory, ASC deemed status), NCQA (health plan accreditation, PCMH recognition). Magnet Recognition (ANCC): nursing excellence
   - Cover: HEDIS domain grid, HCAHPS 8 composites, accreditor comparison table, eCQM structure

13. **"Clinical Quality Measures & Public Reporting"** — Measure development lifecycle: concept → specifications → testing (reliability, validity, feasibility) → endorsement (NQF) → implementation → evaluation → retirement. Measure stewards: CMS, TJC, NCQA, AMA-PCPI, specialty societies. Measure types by reporting mechanism: chart-abstracted, claims-based, eCQM (EHR-extracted), registry-based, survey-based. Public reporting platforms: CMS Care Compare (hospitals, physicians, nursing homes, home health, dialysis, hospice), Leapfrog Hospital Safety Grades, U.S. News rankings, Healthgrades, Vizient Quality & Accountability rankings. Impact of public reporting: transparency, consumer choice, competitive improvement, gaming/teaching to the test risks. Star ratings methodology: CMS 5-star (grouping, latent variable model, clustering), Leapfrog A-F (weighted scoring). HIM's role: abstracting quality data, ensuring coding accuracy for claims-based measures, supporting eCQM data extraction, CDI impact on risk-adjusted measures, POA accuracy for HAC measures
   - Cover: Measure development lifecycle, public reporting platform comparison, star rating methodology concept, HIM data flow to quality measures

### Unit 4: Utilization Management & Risk Management (L14-17)
14. **"Utilization Management & Review"** — Utilization management defined: evaluation of medical necessity, appropriateness, and efficiency of healthcare services. Three types of review: Prospective (pre-admission/pre-service — prior authorization, pre-certification, eligibility verification), Concurrent (during stay — continued stay review, discharge planning, case management, level of care assessment), Retrospective (post-discharge — claims review, pattern analysis, medical necessity audit). InterQual and Milliman Care Guidelines: commercial criteria sets for medical necessity determination. Discharge planning: begins at admission, multidisciplinary team, safe transition, CMS CoP requirements, readmission reduction. Case management: coordination of care, advocacy, resource optimization, CM certifications (CCM, ACM). Observation vs inpatient: 2-midnight rule (CMS — if physician expects stay ≥2 midnights, inpatient appropriate), condition code 44 (inpatient changed to outpatient after utilization review), financial impact on patient and facility. Avoidable days: unnecessary inpatient days, tracking and reduction strategies. Denial management from UM perspective: medical necessity denials, appeal strategies
   - Cover: 3 review types comparison, 2-midnight rule decision tree, observation vs inpatient flow, UM workflow diagram

15. **"Risk Management in Healthcare"** — Risk management defined: systematic process to identify, assess, and reduce risks to patients, staff, visitors, and the organization. Risk identification: incident reports (occurrence reports — not part of medical record, protected in many states), patient complaints, claims data, sentinel events, near misses, safety rounds. Risk assessment: likelihood × severity matrix, risk prioritization. Risk mitigation strategies: avoidance (stop doing the risky activity), reduction (add safeguards — checklists, double-checks, forcing functions), transfer (insurance, contracts, indemnification), acceptance (low likelihood/low impact, document decision). Enterprise Risk Management (ERM): organization-wide, strategic risk identification, COSO framework, risk appetite/tolerance. Professional liability insurance: occurrence-based vs claims-made, tail coverage. Incident reporting: just culture (distinguish human error → console, at-risk behavior → coach, reckless behavior → discipline), non-punitive reporting, de-identified aggregate analysis. Patient safety: culture of safety surveys (AHRQ SOPS), safety huddles, safety event classification (near miss, no-harm event, adverse event, sentinel event). Medical error taxonomy: latent errors (system) vs active errors (individual), Swiss cheese model (Reason)
   - Cover: Risk assessment matrix (likelihood × severity), Swiss cheese model, just culture algorithm, ERM framework

16. **"Patient Safety & Error Prevention"** — Patient safety science: high-reliability organizations (HROs — 5 principles: preoccupation with failure, reluctance to simplify, sensitivity to operations, commitment to resilience, deference to expertise), safety culture vs blame culture. Human factors engineering: designing systems for humans (cognitive load, fatigue, decision fatigue, alarm fatigue), standardization, simplification, forcing functions, constraints. Error prevention strategies: checklists (surgical safety checklist — WHO, "time out"), SBAR communication (Situation-Background-Assessment-Recommendation), read-back for verbal orders/critical test results, medication reconciliation, smart pump technology, bar-code medication administration (BCMA), CPOE (reduces transcription errors). Specific safety initiatives: hand hygiene compliance (WHO 5 Moments), fall prevention (risk assessment, bed alarms, intentional rounding), pressure injury prevention (Braden Scale, turning protocols), VTE prophylaxis, surgical site infection bundles (pre-op antibiotics, skin prep, normothermia, glucose control). TeamSTEPPS (AHRQ): teamwork system for healthcare, mutual support, situation monitoring, communication tools (CUS: Concerned-Uncomfortable-Safety issue, DESC: Describe-Express-Suggest-Consequences)
   - Cover: HRO 5 principles, SBAR template, surgical safety checklist steps, TeamSTEPPS components

17. **"Infection Prevention & HAI Reduction"** — Healthcare-Associated Infections (HAIs): affect 1 in 31 hospital patients on any given day (CDC), $28.4 billion annual cost. Major HAIs: CLABSI (Central Line-Associated Bloodstream Infection), CAUTI (Catheter-Associated Urinary Tract Infection), SSI (Surgical Site Infection), VAE (Ventilator-Associated Event), MRSA bacteremia, C. difficile infection (CDI). NHSN (National Healthcare Safety Network): CDC surveillance system, standardized definitions, standardized infection ratios (SIR = observed/predicted, <1.0 = better than baseline). Prevention bundles: CLABSI bundle (hand hygiene, chlorhexidine skin prep, full barrier precautions, optimal site selection, daily review of line necessity), CAUTI bundle (avoid unnecessary catheters, aseptic insertion, daily assessment for removal, alternatives to indwelling catheters), SSI bundle (pre-op antibiotics within 60 min, appropriate skin prep, normothermia, glucose control). Antimicrobial stewardship: appropriate antibiotic use, resistance prevention, antibiogram, ASP team. Transmission-based precautions: contact, droplet, airborne — in addition to standard precautions. HAI reporting requirements: CMS, state mandates, public reporting. HIM role: accurate coding of HAIs with POA indicators, HAI impact on HAC Reduction Program payment
   - Cover: Major HAI types with prevention bundles SVG, SIR calculation, transmission precaution types, HAI coding and POA impact

### Unit 5: Quality Data & Analytics (L18-20)
18. **"Clinical Documentation Integrity for Quality"** — CDI beyond reimbursement: CDI impacts quality measures, not just DRGs. Severity of Illness (SOI) and Risk of Mortality (ROM): APR-DRG based, drive expected mortality/complication rates. If documentation doesn't capture true acuity → expected rates are artificially low → hospital appears to perform worse than peers. Mortality measures: CMS 30-day mortality (risk-adjusted using claims data), Vizient mortality index (using APR-DRGs). Example: patient with "respiratory failure" coded as J96.00 (unspecified, acute or chronic) vs J96.01 (acute, hypoxic) — specificity impacts APR-DRG SOI/ROM. PSI (Patient Safety Indicators): triggered by specific ICD-10-CM codes, POA status is CRITICAL — if POA=N, counts as hospital-acquired complication. CDI queries for quality: not just CCs/MCCs, also specificity for quality measures (acute vs chronic, type of organism, stage of pressure ulcer, specific type of heart failure). CDI-Quality alignment: CDI reviewers tracking quality indicators alongside reimbursement opportunities. Documentation improvement campaigns: physician education on quality impact, EHR documentation templates for quality-relevant conditions
   - Cover: CDI reimbursement vs quality dual impact diagram, SOI/ROM impact on expected rates, PSI trigger code examples, CDI quality query examples

19. **"Healthcare Data Analytics for Quality Improvement"** — Descriptive analytics: what happened? (dashboards, scorecards, trending, benchmarking). Diagnostic analytics: why did it happen? (drill-down analysis, root cause, correlation). Predictive analytics: what will happen? (risk models, readmission prediction, sepsis early warning, deterioration detection). Prescriptive analytics: what should we do? (clinical decision support, best practice alerts, care pathways). Data visualization for quality: choosing the right chart (line chart for trends, bar chart for comparison, control chart for variation, Pareto for prioritization, funnel plot for benchmarking). Building a quality dashboard: key metrics (mortality, readmission, HAI rates, patient experience, length of stay), visual design principles (RAG status — Red/Amber/Green, sparklines, drill-down capability). Statistical concepts for QI: statistical significance vs clinical significance, run chart rules (shift, trend, run), control chart interpretation (in-control vs out-of-control), sample size considerations. Benchmarking: internal (trending over time), external (peer comparison), competitive (best-in-class). Data integrity: garbage in = garbage out, coding accuracy drives quality data, CDI drives documentation completeness
   - Cover: Analytics maturity model (descriptive → prescriptive), dashboard mockup SVG, chart selection guide, benchmarking types

20. **"Quality Registries & Outcomes Measurement"** — Clinical registries: systematic collection of patient data for defined conditions/procedures. Cancer registries: hospital-based (Commission on Cancer accreditation, cancer registrar role, abstracting, staging — AJCC TNM system, follow-up) + population-based (state registries, SEER program — NCI, NAACCR standards, incidence/survival data). Trauma registries: NTDB (National Trauma Data Bank), ACS trauma center verification levels (I-V), injury severity scoring (ISS, AIS), registry data for trauma system evaluation. Birth defect registries: state-mandated, surveillance, prevention planning. Immunization registries (IIS): Immunization Information Systems, bidirectional exchange with EHR. Transplant registries: UNOS/OPTN, SRTR (Scientific Registry of Transplant Recipients), outcomes tracking. Specialty registries: STS (cardiac surgery), NSQIP (surgical quality, ACS), Get With the Guidelines (AHA stroke/heart). Outcomes measurement: patient-reported outcomes (PROs, PROMs — PROMIS system), functional status measures, patient-centered outcomes research (PCORI). Registry data uses: benchmarking, quality improvement, research, public health, public reporting
   - Cover: Registry type comparison table, cancer staging TNM concept, trauma center levels, registry data flow diagram

### Unit 6: Capstone (L21-22)
21. **"Building a Quality Improvement Project"** — Full QI project simulation from start to finish: Step 1: Identify opportunity (using data — high readmission rate for heart failure). Step 2: Assemble team (champion, team leader, members, subject matter experts — include HIM for data/coding). Step 3: Define the project (aim statement, SMART goals, scope, measures — outcome/process/balancing). Step 4: Analyze current state (process map, data collection, baseline measurement, root cause analysis — fishbone, 5 Whys). Step 5: Design interventions (evidence-based practices, brainstorming, prioritization matrix, pilot design). Step 6: Implement (PDSA cycles, small tests of change, track with run charts). Step 7: Measure results (statistical analysis, control charts, pre vs post comparison). Step 8: Sustain (standardize successful changes, control plan, ongoing monitoring, spread to other units). Step 9: Report (storyboard, presentation to leadership, lessons learned). Storyboard template: background, aim, measures, interventions, results, lessons, next steps. HIM-led QI examples: coding accuracy improvement, chart completion turnaround, CDI query response rate, ROI turnaround, denial reduction
   - Cover: 9-step QI project flowchart, storyboard template SVG, PDSA tracking chart, aim statement formula

22. **"Capstone: Integrated Quality Case Studies"** — Case 1: Hospital receives 3-star CMS rating — analyze which quality domains are weak, identify data issues (coding accuracy? POA errors? CDI gaps?), design improvement plan targeting specific measures. Case 2: Sentinel event (wrong-site surgery) — conduct RCA, identify system failures, develop corrective action plan, report to TJC, implement Universal Protocol improvements. Case 3: CLABSI rate spike in ICU — use control chart to confirm special cause, investigate root causes, implement evidence-based bundle, monitor with run chart, present results. Case 4: CMS HRRP penalty notification — analyze readmission data by condition, identify root causes (discharge planning gaps, medication reconciliation failures, inadequate follow-up), design multidisciplinary reduction program. Case 5: TJC survey preparation — evaluate compliance with NPSGs, prepare tracer-ready documentation, conduct mock survey, address deficiencies. For each: apply appropriate QI tools, use data to drive decisions, connect HIM role to quality outcomes.
   - Cover: Case analysis frameworks, QI tool selection for each case, data visualization for results, corrective action plan templates

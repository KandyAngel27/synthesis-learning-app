## Lesson Plan (28 Lessons)

### Unit 1: Word-Building Foundations (L1-5)
1. **"Introduction to Medical Language"** — Why medical terminology exists: precision in communication, Greek & Latin origins (~75% of medical terms from Greek/Latin), standardized meanings across all healthcare settings. How medical terms are built: word root (core meaning — cardi = heart, hepat = liver, neur = nerve), combining vowel (usually "o" — connects root to suffix or another root, used when suffix begins with consonant, NOT used when suffix begins with vowel), combining form (root + combining vowel = cardi/o, hepat/o, neur/o), prefix (beginning modifier — pre-, post-, hyper-, hypo-), suffix (ending that gives medical meaning — -itis = inflammation, -ectomy = surgical removal, -ology = study of). Pronunciation rules: "c" before e/i = soft "s" sound (cerebral), "c" before a/o/u = hard "k" (cardiac), "g" before e/i = soft "j" (generic), "ps" = "s" (psychology), "pn" = "n" (pneumonia), "pt" = "t" (pterygoid), "rh" = "r" (rhinoplasty), silent letters. Plural forms: -a → -ae (vertebra/vertebrae), -is → -es (diagnosis/diagnoses), -ix/-ex → -ices (appendix/appendices), -on → -a (ganglion/ganglia), -um → -a (bacterium/bacteria), -us → -i (bronchus/bronchi). Eponyms: medical terms named after people (Parkinson's, Alzheimer's, Crohn's) — trend toward descriptive terms
   - Cover: Word-building anatomy diagram (prefix + root + combining vowel + suffix), Greek/Latin origin examples, pronunciation rules reference, plural formation table

2. **"Prefixes: Position, Direction & Number"** — Position/location prefixes: epi- (upon, above — epidermis), hypo- (under, below — hypodermic), sub- (under — subcutaneous), supra- (above — suprapubic), infra- (below — infraorbital), intra- (within — intravenous), inter- (between — intercostal), peri- (around — pericardium), retro- (behind — retroperitoneal), endo- (within — endoscopy), exo-/ecto- (outside — exocrine, ectopic), trans- (across — transdermal), para- (beside/near — parathyroid), ab- (away from — abduction), ad- (toward — adduction). Direction prefixes: ante- (before/front — anterior, antepartum), post- (after/behind — posterior, postoperative), pre- (before — prenatal), pro- (before/forward — prognosis), lateral/medial/proximal/distal as directional terms. Number/quantity prefixes: uni-/mono- (one), bi-/di- (two), tri- (three), quadri- (four), multi-/poly- (many), hemi-/semi- (half), pan- (all — pandemic). Size/comparison prefixes: macro- (large), micro- (small), mega-/megalo- (enlarged), oligo- (scanty/few). Clinical application: build 20+ medical terms using these prefixes with common roots
   - Cover: Position prefix body diagram (showing where epi-/hypo-/sub-/supra- point on body), number prefix table, direction prefix arrows SVG, clinical term building exercise

3. **"Prefixes: Negation, Time & Condition"** — Negation prefixes: a-/an- (without — apnea = without breathing, anemia = without blood), anti- (against — antibiotic, antihypertensive), contra- (against/opposite — contraindicated, contralateral), de- (removal/down — dehydration, decompression), dys- (difficult/painful/abnormal — dyspnea, dysphagia, dysuria), in-/im- (not — incurable, immobile), mal- (bad — malignant, malabsorption), non- (not — noncompliant). Time prefixes: ante-/pre- (before — antepartum, prenatal), post- (after — postmortem, postoperative), neo- (new — neonatal, neoplasm), brady- (slow — bradycardia), tachy- (fast — tachycardia), re- (again — remission, recurrence), chrono- (time — chronic). Condition/state prefixes: eu- (good/normal — eupnea, euthyroid), pseudo- (false — pseudocyst), auto- (self — autoimmune), homeo-/homo- (same — homeostasis), hetero- (different — heterogeneous), iso- (equal — isotonic). Color prefixes: cyan/o- (blue — cyanosis), erythr/o- (red — erythrocyte), leuk/o- (white — leukocyte), melan/o- (black — melanoma), xanth/o- (yellow — xanthoma), cirrh/o- (orange-yellow — cirrhosis). Compound prefix practice: combine multiple prefixes — antihypertensive (anti- + hyper- + tens-), pseudohypoparathyroidism
   - Cover: Negation prefix comparison grid, time prefix timeline, color prefix spectrum SVG, compound term deconstruction examples

4. **"Suffixes: Conditions, Diseases & Symptoms"** — Condition/disease suffixes: -itis (inflammation — appendicitis, bronchitis, hepatitis), -osis (abnormal condition — stenosis, cyanosis, neurosis), -emia (blood condition — anemia, septicemia, hyperglycemia), -pathy (disease — neuropathy, cardiomyopathy, nephropathy), -algia/-dynia (pain — neuralgia, myalgia, pleurodynia), -cele (hernia/protrusion — cystocele, rectocele, meningocele), -ectasis (dilation/stretching — bronchiectasis, atelectasis), -edema (swelling — lymphedema), -emesis (vomiting — hematemesis), -genesis (origin/producing — pathogenesis, carcinogenesis), -iasis (pathological condition — cholelithiasis, nephrolithiasis), -ism (condition/theory — hypothyroidism, embolism), -itis vs -osis: inflammation vs degenerative/abnormal condition (arthritis = joint inflammation, arthrosis = joint degeneration), -lysis (destruction/breakdown — hemolysis, fibrinolysis, paralysis), -malacia (softening — osteomalacia, chondromalacia), -megaly (enlargement — cardiomegaly, hepatomegaly, splenomegaly), -necrosis (tissue death), -oma (tumor — carcinoma, melanoma, lipoma, -oma doesn't always mean cancer: hematoma, fibroma are benign), -penia (deficiency — leukopenia, thrombocytopenia), -phobia (fear/aversion — claustrophobia, agoraphobia), -plegia (paralysis — hemiplegia, quadriplegia, paraplegia), -ptosis (drooping — nephroptosis, blepharoptosis), -rrhage/-rrhagia (bursting forth/hemorrhage — hemorrhage, menorrhagia), -rrhea (flow/discharge — diarrhea, rhinorrhea), -sclerosis (hardening — arteriosclerosis, atherosclerosis, multiple sclerosis), -spasm (involuntary contraction — bronchospasm, vasospasm), -stasis (stopping/controlling — hemostasis, metastasis = spreading/changing place), -trophy (development — hypertrophy, atrophy, dystrophy)
   - Cover: Top 20 condition suffixes with examples grid, -itis vs -osis comparison, -oma benign vs malignant distinction, suffix categorization (pain/size/function/condition)

5. **"Suffixes: Procedures, Specialists & Instruments"** — Surgical/procedure suffixes: -ectomy (surgical removal — appendectomy, cholecystectomy, mastectomy), -ostomy (creating an opening — colostomy, tracheostomy, ileostomy), -otomy (cutting into — craniotomy, thoracotomy, laparotomy), -ectomy vs -otomy vs -ostomy (remove vs cut into vs create opening — critical distinction), -plasty (surgical repair/reconstruction — rhinoplasty, arthroplasty, angioplasty), -pexy (surgical fixation — orchiopexy, nephropexy), -rrhaphy (suturing — herniorrhaphy, neurorrhaphy), -tripsy (crushing — lithotripsy), -desis (binding/fixation — arthrodesis), -centesis (surgical puncture to aspirate — amniocentesis, thoracentesis, paracentesis, arthrocentesis), -clasis (intentional breaking — osteoclasis). Diagnostic suffixes: -scopy (visual examination — endoscopy, colonoscopy, bronchoscopy, arthroscopy), -gram (record/image — electrocardiogram, mammogram, angiogram), -graph (recording instrument — electrocardiograph), -graphy (process of recording — radiography, ultrasonography, tomography), -meter (measuring instrument — thermometer, spirometer), -metry (process of measuring — spirometry, audiometry, oximetry). Specialist/study suffixes: -ologist (specialist — cardiologist, neurologist, pathologist), -ology (study of — cardiology, neurology, pathology), -iatrics/-iatry (medical treatment — pediatrics, psychiatry, geriatrics), -ist (specialist — dentist, pharmacist, therapist), -ian (specialist — physician, dietitian, clinician). Diminutive suffixes: -ole/-ule (small — arteriole, venule, tubule), -icle (small — ventricle, follicle)
   - Cover: Surgical suffix trio (-ectomy/-otomy/-ostomy) comparison SVG, diagnostic suffix hierarchy, -gram/-graph/-graphy distinction, specialist suffix examples by medical field
